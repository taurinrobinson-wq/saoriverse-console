Taurin — you’re right to pause here. The player’s initial knowledge state sets the tone for how they
experience Velinor. Let’s break down the options and implications:

🧩 Player Knowledge Options

1. No Memory (Tabula Rasa) Pros:

Maximizes mystery and discovery.

Aligns with NPCs who also struggle with fragments.

Makes glyph collection feel like personal restoration.

Cons:

Contradicts the marketplace setup (society has already begun to rebuild).

Risks player frustration if they feel too unmoored.

Best Use: Works if the player is newly awakened from deeper Corelink integration — they’re behind
the curve compared to survivors who’ve already started rebuilding.

2. Partial Memory (Fragmented Identity) Pros:

Matches the world’s state: survivors remember enough to barter, rebuild, but not their full lineage.

Lets the player recall their name and basic survival skills, but not deeper emotional history.

Creates resonance with NPCs — everyone is ashamed of what they’ve forgotten, but the player is
uniquely able to recover glyphs.

Cons:

Requires careful calibration: too much memory and the glyph hunt feels redundant; too little and it
feels incoherent.

Best Use: Player remembers their name, marketplace basics, and survival context, but not their
emotional lineage or pre‑collapse identity. Glyphs restore those deeper layers.

3. Full Memory (Outlier Survivor) Pros:

Makes the player unique — they are one of the few who disconnected before collapse (like Saori).

Explains why they can guide others.

Cons:

Risks breaking the empathy mechanic: if the player already knows everything, NPC resonance arcs lose
weight.

Could make the game feel more expositional than experiential.

Best Use: Works if the player is positioned as a keeper of forbidden knowledge — but then the quest
shifts from discovery to deciding what truths to share.

🎭 Narrative Recommendation Given your marketplace setup (time has passed, nomadic structures exist),
the most coherent starting point is Partial Memory:

Player remembers their name and basic survival context.

They know enough to navigate the marketplace and barter.

What they don’t remember are their emotional lineage, sacred vows, and pre‑collapse connections —
those are locked in glyphs.

This makes glyph collection feel like recovering who they are and who others were, not just abstract
lore.

🌌 Framing in Story Opening scene: Player wakes in the marketplace, recognized by others as “one who
listens.”

They know their name, but when asked about their past, they falter.

Saori appears later to reveal: “You remember enough to walk, but not enough to belong. The glyphs
will teach you.”

This way, the player is anchored but incomplete — coherent enough for the marketplace, mysterious
enough for the glyph hunt, and narratively aligned with your empathy‑driven mechanics.
