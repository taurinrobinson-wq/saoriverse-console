Yes. That is a real plot hole, but I think it reveals the missing piece in the history of Corelink rather than breaking the premise.

The chambers cannot have been constructed specifically to repair Velinor’s post-collapse psyche. The cleanest answer is:

The chambers were always designed for psychological integration
The chambers do not recognize Velinor’s personal fragments. They recognize the emotional encoding system that Corelink already used.

Before the collapse, Corelink needed some way to:

identify emotional and mnemonic patterns;
separate unstable or conflicting patterns;
test whether several patterns could coexist coherently;
recombine compatible fragments into a stable higher-order structure;
prevent corrupted emotional data from being reintroduced into the larger network.
The glyphs were the readable symbolic form of that system. A Transcendence Chamber was therefore a Corelink integration and resonance facility, perhaps originally used for research, calibration, therapy, or network maintenance.

The chamber is effectively saying:

These emotional patterns are compatible and, when resonated together, produce this integrated state.

It is not saying:

Please insert three pieces of Velinor.

Velinor fragmented according to the system she built
That is the causal bridge you need.

When Velinor entered Corelink and absorbed or redirected the overload, her psyche did not fracture randomly. It fractured along the same emotional classifications and associative structures that Corelink used to encode human experience.

In other words, Corelink’s architecture became the fracture lines.

That is why:

Velinor’s fragments manifest as glyphs;
fragments become anchored at distributed Corelink nodes;
only certain fragments can be successfully combined;
the old chambers can process them;
the resulting Transcendence glyphs restore more complex portions of Velinor’s mind.
The chambers predate the injury, but the injury occurred inside the system for which the chambers were built.

That feels less like coincidence and more like consequence.

The specific combinations represent integrated psychological functions
I would avoid treating the combinations as arbitrary recipes. Each Transcendence glyph should correspond to a complex emotional capacity that requires several constituent states.

For example, purely illustratively:



Grief + Attachment + Acceptance
        ↓
Integrated Mourning
Or:



Fear + Responsibility + Resolve
        ↓
Moral Courage
 
Velinor does not regain three isolated emotions. The chamber converts those fragments into a stable psychological relationship among them. That restored structure can then resonate back through Corelink and reintegrate into Velinor.

This gives “Transcendence” a precise meaning. It is not that the component glyphs disappear into a stronger magical glyph. They become an organized emotional capacity greater than their isolated parts.

Why Lioren must physically choose the glyphs
The chamber can perform resonance, but it cannot determine which fragments belong together because the fragments have become detached from their original context.

Lioren supplies what Corelink lacks:

empathy;
contextual understanding gained from the residents;
recognition of emotional relationships;
freedom from the compromised implant network.
The player is not solving an arbitrary combination lock. The player is reconstructing the relationships between pieces of a person.

That also explains why Corelink could not simply repair Velinor automatically. It retained the machinery for integration, but not the intact perspective necessary to interpret what the fragments meant.

The reveal at the end
Throughout most of the game, the player can reasonably believe the Transcendence glyphs are being created for some external purpose. Perhaps they appear to be repairing the local nodes, stabilizing corrupted memories, or unlocking access toward the Corelink center.

Only later does the player understand:

Every successful integration has been transmitted through the surviving network and incorporated into Velinor.

The chambers were not built for her, but because she sacrificed herself through the system, she became the damaged network they were repairing.

I would make this the canon rather than introducing a prophecy, post-collapse construction, or machinery that somehow manufactured chambers in anticipation of Lioren. It turns the apparent coincidence into a direct consequence of how Corelink encoded minds.
