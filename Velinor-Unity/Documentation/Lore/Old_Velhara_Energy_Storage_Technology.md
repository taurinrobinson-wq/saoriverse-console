# Old Velhara Energy Storage Technology

## Purpose

This document establishes the technological basis for electrical energy storage in Old Velhara and the origin of the salvage item broadly known as a **Power Cell**.

The technology should feel plausibly descended from real-world battery research without requiring the game to explain its chemistry to the player. Most people living in 2125 no longer possess the technical knowledge necessary to identify the original technologies precisely.

---

## Design Principle

Old Velhara did not rely on a single universal battery chemistry.

By 2100, energy storage had diversified according to application. Different technologies were used for portable equipment, buildings, transportation infrastructure, emergency systems, and large-scale stationary storage.

For gameplay purposes, Lioren categorizes surviving portable energy modules simply as:

**Power Cell**

This is a functional category rather than the original technical name of a particular device.

---

# Portable Power Cells

## Canonical Concept

Portable equipment in Old Velhara commonly used rugged, standardized energy modules based on advanced **sodium-derived rechargeable battery technology**.

The exact chemistry is intentionally unspecified.

The technology represents approximately 75 years of fictional development beyond contemporary sodium-ion research. It should therefore not be treated simply as a 2020s sodium-ion battery placed in a futuristic casing.

The important characteristics are:

- Rechargeable
- Designed for long service life
- Modular and replaceable
- Produced in several physical formats
- Highly sealed against environmental exposure
- Intended for industrial, scientific, emergency, and portable equipment
- Designed around standardized electrical interfaces
- More tolerant of abuse than early twenty-first-century consumer batteries
- Capable of retaining some useful functionality after decades under favorable conditions

Whether a particular recovered cell remains usable depends upon its condition and where it spent the intervening 25 years.

---

## Visual Design

Power Cells should share an identifiable **Old Velharan technological language** without all looking identical.

Possible forms include:

- Flat rectangular equipment modules
- Cylindrical cells
- Long narrow cartridge cells
- Compact reinforced battery packs
- Specialized cells removed from machinery
- Damaged modules with partially exposed internal components

Shared visual characteristics can include:

- Durable metallic or high-performance polymer housings
- Protected electrical contacts
- Mechanical alignment features
- Orange industrial identification markings
- Dark neutral structural components
- Muted turquoise or cyan internal materials
- Simple geometric manufacturer or system markings
- Replaceable-module construction
- Scratches, oxidation, grime, damaged seals, and environmental wear

The turquoise material visible in some Power Cells does **not** need to glow. The visual design should communicate stored technological potential without resembling a conventional science-fiction glowing energy crystal.

Different Power Cells may originally have served completely different machines.

Gameplay nevertheless classifies them together because they fulfill the same recovered function.

# Knowledge After the Corelink Disaster

Most inhabitants no longer understand the underlying chemistry or engineering.

Over the subsequent 25 years, survivors have rediscovered the practical properties of surviving technology through experimentation, scavenging, observation, and knowledge passed between people.

The term **Power Cell** is therefore survivor terminology.

A person may know:

- this object stores power;
- these contacts connect to electrical devices;
- some cells are dead;
- some still work;
- particular combinations of salvaged components can make useful equipment.

The same person does not necessarily know:

- the original battery chemistry;
- how the cell was manufactured;
- the original product designation;
- why its internal components work;
- how Old Velhara's electrical infrastructure was engineered.

This distinction is important to the setting.

**Practical knowledge has survived more effectively than theoretical knowledge.**

Someone like Willy may be highly competent at identifying, testing, connecting, and repurposing Power Cells despite having little understanding of electrochemistry.

A representative exchange could therefore be:

**Lioren:** "What is this?"

**Willy:** "Power Cell."

**Lioren:** "What does it do?"

**Willy:** "Makes things work."

This should characterize the technological knowledge of the setting more accurately than lengthy exposition.

# Relationship to Crafting

The gameplay material **Power Cell** represents any sufficiently intact portable Old Velharan energy module suitable for repurposing.

The first important use is the **Portable Light**:

- Electronic Parts ×1
- Power Cell ×1
- Wire ×1

The recipe is intentionally crude compared with Old Velharan engineering.

Lioren and Willy are not manufacturing a new Power Cell. They are assembling surviving technological components into a new device whose function they understand.

This supports the broader crafting philosophy of the game:

**People have forgotten how the old world made things, but they have learned what some of its pieces can still do.**

---

# Stationary Energy Storage

Old Velhara's large-scale infrastructure should not simply consist of enormous versions of the portable Power Cell.

The city used different energy-storage technologies according to scale and purpose.

Large installations may therefore contain the remnants of technologies conceptually descended from:

- Flow batteries
- Iron-based storage
- Zinc-based storage
- Sodium-based stationary storage
- Thermal or other industrial energy-storage systems

These systems can appear in:

- Engineering areas
- Utility spaces
- Environmental-control facilities
- Emergency infrastructure
- Research laboratories
- Service tunnels
- Corelink support infrastructure

Their surviving components need not become separate inventory materials unless gameplay requires them.

---

# Flow Battery Visual Language

Flow-battery technology is particularly useful as inspiration for ruined Old Velhara infrastructure.

Instead of looking like giant batteries, these systems can visually resemble industrial chemical-processing equipment:

- Large storage vessels
- Pumps
- Pipes
- Valves
- Fluid reservoirs
- Monitoring equipment
- Exchange or reaction assemblies
- Service access points

This provides environmental variety while allowing Old Velhara's energy infrastructure to feel technologically advanced without relying entirely on glowing futuristic objects.

Any century-scale durability or unusual electrolyte properties depicted in the game should be understood as fictional advances developed between the present and 2100 rather than properties asserted of present-day systems.

---

# Alternative Technologies

Other battery technologies may have existed throughout Old Velhara.

## Zinc-Based Systems

Zinc-derived storage can provide another technological lineage for specialized or stationary equipment.

## Iron-Based Systems

Iron-based technologies are particularly appropriate as inspiration for large, inexpensive stationary storage where mass and volume were less important than durability or material availability.

## Aluminum and Magnesium Systems

Advanced metal-based systems may have existed for specialized high-output or emergency applications.

These should remain background technology unless a specific story or gameplay purpose requires greater detail.

---

# Environmental Survival

Power Cells found in 2125 should not all be functional merely because Old Velharan technology was advanced.

The condition of a recovered cell can depend upon environmental circumstances.

A cell discovered:

- inside a relatively dry sealed cabinet,
- within abandoned machinery,
- inside a protected service area,
- in a preserved Corelink facility,

may plausibly be in much better condition than one that spent decades directly exposed to rain and vegetation.

This distinction should appear visually.

Some Power Cell props can be:

- relatively intact,
- heavily weathered but functional,
- cracked,
- corroded,
- partially dismantled,
- completely dead environmental debris.

Only usable examples need to become inventory pickups.

This allows the ruins to contain considerable technological debris without suggesting that everything manufactured in 2100 somehow survived 25 years of abandonment.

---

# Environmental Storytelling Rule

Technology should generally become **less intelligible to the characters as it becomes more intelligible to the player visually**.

Players may recognize:

- batteries,
- electrical distribution,
- sensors,
- communications equipment,
- pumps,
- mechanical systems,

even when Lioren lacks the vocabulary to identify their exact purpose.

The game should avoid excessive technical exposition.

Old Velhara should feel like a civilization whose machines remain physically present while the knowledge that explained those machines has largely disappeared.

---

# Canon Summary

**Old Velhara, circa 2100:**  
Used numerous mature energy-storage technologies appropriate to different applications.

**Portable equipment:**  
Frequently powered by advanced sodium-derived modular energy cells.

**Large infrastructure:**  
Used multiple stationary technologies, potentially including flow, sodium, zinc, and iron-based systems.

**After the Corelink disaster:**  
Technical understanding was largely lost, while practical knowledge gradually re-emerged.

**2125 survivor terminology:**  
Usable portable energy modules are collectively called **Power Cells**, regardless of their original model or exact application.

**Gameplay philosophy:**  
Power Cell is an inventory category, not one specific historical product.

**Visual philosophy:**  
Power Cells should look related without looking identical. They are surviving pieces of a lost technological ecosystem, not standardized videogame pickups.

**Narrative principle:**

> The people of Velhara do not need to remember how the old world worked in order to discover what pieces of it can still do.
